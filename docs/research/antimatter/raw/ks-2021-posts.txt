Corporations

Corporations are the fabric of the economical activity of the galaxy, they are responsible for interplanetary trade and other activities.

The universe is now teeming with activity, each character employed by a corporation is now minding his own business, indeed, nothing is spawned out of thin air, there is always a reason for something to exist, and always an AI to drive it (which is both false and true for tesla vehicles).

Corporations have a primary focus and sometimes a secondary one, this defines the commercial strategy, a trader corporation focuses on buying cargo and profit from trades opportunities, a scientist orders civilian ships and conducts deep space exploration, to make money out of discoveries of planets and POI. A manufacturer controls shipyards and sells manufactured ships or gets a cut when another corporation or a character sells a ship located in the space station. A Fighter corporation acts as a mercenary and will scout or "search and destroy" ships hostile to its faction. Financial corporation trades shares of profitable corporations in the hope of dividends.

A corporation also has a legal stance, either Lawful, Shady, or Outlaw (or ones that should be outlawed, such as Microsoft with their forced restarts), this determines the type of activities, for example as an outlaw trader, the corporation will conduct smuggling operations, for an outlaw fighter corporation, it will be a pirate.

A corporation is usually associated with a faction and is a source of taxes for the latter. However such organizations are usually very autonomous in such an anarcho-capitalist model. Corporations exist in an open market, with different ranks of employees and representatives, each having a salary quite proportional to the corporation's wealth. Corporations can be exchanged through shares, however, to prevent exploits and market-making shenanigans that would unbalance the financial aspect of the game, and crash the economy (hum hum.. real-life hum.. hum) I made sure to have a high transaction spread. In other words, you sell at much lower prices and you buy at much higher prices.

===== [2021-05-13] Space exploration =====
Now that ships, modules, and navigation are polished I have finally all the foundations for space exploration.

Sensors

Most ships can be equipped with a sensor, which has a combined purpose of detecting planets and moons, spotting ships, and survey the planet's surface.  All sensors "ping" every 30 seconds.

Surveying a system

The combined sensor strength of each sensor increases the chance and range of spotting a body (be it a planet, a moon, or an asteroid). the chance of detecting is based on the body's albedo (how reflective its surface is) the radius and the distance to the ship. So spotting a planet can be tricky, especially considering that they gravitate around their star.  Planet in the outer rim of a system can be particularly hard to spot. Gaz giants are obviously easy to spot and are usually a good starting point since some moons are often gravitating around it.

[img]
Entering an unexplored system. Some planets have already been spotted.

Surveying a planet

If you have found a planet with an interesting classification, you may want to investigate it a little more.

By orbiting the planet, the ship will automatically survey the surface's regions.  Sensor strength plays a role too but also the diversity of sensors will add a bonus, a combination of smaller sensors will increase the chance of surveying a region.

[img]
Orbiting the planet with an exploration ship

The chance of surveying a region is directly tied to the cloud cover and the radius of the planet. It can take a lot of time for a cloudy planet. 

A surveyed region will display a lot of useful details, but the player can land in an unsurveyed region as well, this will be covered in one of the next updates when surface exploration will be expanded.

This planet has a cloud cover of 80% and surveying it takes a lot of time.

Navigation

Navigation is essential on this immense scale. Going to hyperspace is limited by the star's gravity well (which depends on the star's mass). For a star like the Sun, it becomes dangerous to jump to hyperspace within 5 AU (around the distance of Jupiter). Post-Scriptum, answering Philippe's question :

Philippe Malacher asked a question on the last update that deserves to be elaborated here :

***

--"More precisely, are traders always AI controlled? Or can the player own trader ships or even extra corporations? Planetside, can you order specifically a certain material to a given corporation, like a trade deal (say deliver me 50 steel every month), or this is a "series of one shot", you have to ask at regular interval for something and perhaps, one trader will deliver them to you? Traders are indeed AI controlled, but so far the player can control a spaceship himself by clicking on it. this is sort of a god mode for now, and in the future it will be necessary to be physically in the ship to control it.

The trade model works with an order system under the hood, but the supply/demand and price variation dynamic is a virtual representation by itself. This means that it is totally possible to inflate or deflate the virtual supply/demand and therefore induce or not more orders.

For now, there is no UI implementation to illustrate this but in the future, the player will be able to control manually the buying price and the volume needed and the traders will sort out themselves if it is a good deal, with the current model. I want to avoid micro-management.

Player can indeed control a corporation, they actually own one at the start of the game (each agent having a starship start with a corporation at the beginning of the game)

Corporation simulation is quite barebone for now so I won't talk a lot about it, but you can also exchange shares of any corporation. These are low liquidity shares with high spread to avoid speculation and exploits.

Ecology 

Ecology plays an important role in Antimatter. For instance, it individually affects the vegetation.  Ruining the fragile ecosystem with overexploitation could lead to disastrous economical and social repercussions in the long run.

Flora 

Flora has been expanded with more types of plants and especially more behaviors associated that affect their growth behavior. Flora growth and health are affected by soil type, luminosity, moisture, temperature, and soil pollution. Vegetation also has a beneficial effect on soil and air pollution.

Of of the most important things is how vegetations affect resource production for the controlling settlement. Each mature flora produces a set of harvestable resources that can be exploited by the neighboring residents or industry. The population naturally exploits the controlled biomes without a single input from the player. 

It is planned to make flora an important part of the economy as it provides different kinds of food that can be transformed into finer food, provides special molecules difficult to synthesize usable for (il)legal drugs, basic materials for construction, or other intermediate resources.

Population needs

Each population  has 3 types of needs :

Vital: Needed to be kept alive. For the only available race so far (human) this is only food.

Basic: Provides happiness. Races and jobs influence such basic needs.

Luxury: Provide happiness * basic needs. Therefore a satisfying luxury needs without fulfilling the basic ones won't have an effect on happiness. Races and jobs influence such needs.

Needs can notably be affected by policies and is entirely dependant on race, jobs (and social class).

Expanding the number of recipes

Antimatter comes with a crafting system, primarily used for industries (but also prototyped for player crafting). If you are familiar with Factorio or Anno, you will understand what is my goal in terms of supply chain complexity.  Civilisations in Antimatter spans over numerous planets with various characteristics. For now, the trade dynamic is shallowly simulated with the possibility to trade with natives cities (if any) and the rest of the galaxy as a unified market.

Currently, Antimatter features 63 types of resources and 53 crafting recipes.

Economy

Civilian occupation is an inefficient but very flexible way to produce goods without a supporting industry. Every unemployed person produces a small amount of GDP that can be used for this. This gives incentive to your population to focus on producing by themself certain categories of goods.

I will once again mention the Anno series, and specifically the older titles by noting the similarities of my system with theirs. Population needs come with a variety of goods, influencing health and happiness, however, the crucial benefit of a variety of consumable goods is the opportunity of profiteering with sales taxes. Each consumed item by the population is taxed by the state (you). A diversified economy and trade relation is therefore key to a healthy economy.
